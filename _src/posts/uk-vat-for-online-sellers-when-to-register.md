Expanding an e-commerce store into the United Kingdom gives sellers access to a major consumer market, but it also brings specific tax responsibilities. Whether you run a storefront from within the country or ship products from abroad, HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) enforces strict rules governing Value Added Tax. This guide explains when online sellers must register for UK VAT, how cross-border consignment rules work, and what obligations apply to domestic and overseas traders.

## When does a UK business need to register for VAT?

A business established in the UK must register for UK VAT if its cumulative taxable turnover exceeds £90,000 over any rolling 12-month period, or if it expects taxable turnover to pass £90,000 in the next 30 days alone. Businesses trading below this threshold are not required to register, but may choose to do so voluntarily.

For companies based in the UK, tracking turnover is an ongoing requirement rather than an annual accounting exercise. The £90,000 threshold applies on a rolling 12-month basis. At the end of each calendar month, you must calculate your total taxable sales over the preceding 12 months. If that cumulative figure passes £90,000, you have reached the threshold under the [HMRC registration rules](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat/vat-notice-7001-supplement--2).

When you cross the rolling 12-month threshold, you must register with HMRC within 30 days of the end of the month in which you exceeded the limit. Your effective date of registration becomes the first day of the second month following that threshold breach. For example, if your rolling taxable turnover crosses £90,000 on 15 May, you must notify HMRC by 30 June, and your registration takes effect from 1 July.

A separate forward-looking rule applies if you anticipate rapid sales growth. If you realise at any point that your taxable turnover will exceed £90,000 in the next 30 days alone, you must register within 30 days of forming that expectation. Under this rule, your VAT registration takes effect from the date you formed the expectation, rather than at the end of the month.

If trading conditions change and your sales drop, businesses can apply for deregistration. You may cancel your UK VAT registration if your taxable turnover falls below the deregistration threshold of £88,000 across a 12-month period.

## When do overseas online sellers have to register for UK VAT?

Overseas sellers face a nil registration threshold. If your business has no physical establishment or central administration in the UK, HM Revenue and Customs classifies you as a Non-Established Taxable Person. Registration can therefore be required from the first taxable UK supply, regardless of turnover. Sellers making only zero-rated deemed supplies through an online marketplace may apply for exemption from registration, as explained below.

![Illustration of cargo containers and postal boxes at a distribution terminal.](/img/insights/uk-vat-for-online-sellers-when-to-register-2.webp)

Under HMRC definitions, having a UK business establishment requires a location where essential management decisions are taken and central administration is carried out. Alternatively, it requires a permanent physical presence equipped with the human and technical resources necessary to make or receive taxable supplies. Operating through a registered office, a virtual address, a forwarding postal box, or holding inventory in a third-party warehouse does not create a UK business establishment under [VAT Notice 700/1](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat/vat-notice-7001-supplement--2).

Because non-established sellers do not receive the £90,000 domestic allowance, the threshold is £0. If an overseas business expects to make a taxable supply in the UK within the next 30 days, it must register within 30 days of forming that expectation. Registration takes effect from the date that expectation arose.

This nil threshold applies directly to any overseas seller holding stock within the UK at the time of sale. If you import goods into a UK warehouse or fulfilment centre and sell them directly from that local inventory to UK shoppers, you are making a domestic taxable supply. You must register for UK VAT, charge UK VAT on the transaction, and remit that tax directly to HMRC.

## How do consignment values affect goods shipped from abroad?

Consignment value determines whether UK supply VAT applies at checkout or at customs. For direct sales of goods dispatched from abroad in parcels valued at £135 or less, sellers must charge UK supply VAT at the point of sale. Goods sent in consignments over £135 face standard import VAT and customs declarations at the border.

When an overseas merchant sells directly to a consumer in Great Britain without using an online marketplace, consignments valued at £135 or less bypass border import VAT. Instead, HMRC treats the transaction as a domestic supply made at the point of sale. According to [official guidance on direct overseas sales](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-and-overseas-goods-sold-directly-to-customers-in-the-uk), the overseas merchant must be registered for UK VAT to collect this tax at checkout and pay it through a UK VAT return.

The rules shift slightly when selling low-value parcels directly to business clients. If an overseas seller ships a consignment valued at £135 or less to a UK VAT-registered business, the seller does not collect VAT at checkout, provided the commercial customer provides a valid UK VAT registration number. In this scenario, the business customer accounts for the VAT internally using the reverse charge mechanism, and the seller notes the customer's VAT number on the commercial invoice.

For orders shipped from overseas in consignments exceeding £135 in value, point-of-sale supply VAT does not apply. Instead, these parcels undergo standard customs clearance when entering the UK border. Normal customs declarations, applicable customs duties, and import VAT are assessed at importation rather than collected by the merchant at checkout.

## What are the VAT rules when selling through online marketplaces?

When overseas businesses sell via an online marketplace, the marketplace operator collects and remits consumer VAT for imports up to £135 and for stock stored locally in Great Britain. Sellers making only zero-rated deemed supplies may register or apply to HMRC for exemption from registration; this exemption is not automatic.

![Illustration of an e-commerce fulfilment warehouse with rows of boxed goods.](/img/insights/uk-vat-for-online-sellers-when-to-register-3.webp)

To ensure tax compliance across digital platforms, the UK introduced marketplace facilitation rules. Under [HMRC marketplace guidelines](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-and-overseas-goods-sold-to-customers-in-the-uk-using-online-marketplaces), an online marketplace becomes liable for VAT in two distinct overseas trading scenarios:

- **Consignments of £135 or less sent from abroad**: If an overseas merchant sells goods shipped from outside the UK to a consumer in Great Britain through a marketplace platform, the marketplace operator accounts for the VAT at the point of sale.
- **Goods located in the UK at sale**: If an overseas business stores inventory inside a UK facility and sells goods of any value to private consumers via a marketplace, the marketplace operator is responsible for charging and remitting consumer VAT at checkout.

When a marketplace accounts for VAT on goods already stored in the UK, HMRC splits the transaction into two legs. The transaction between the overseas merchant and the online marketplace is classified as a zero-rated deemed supply. The marketplace then makes a deemed retail supply to the final consumer and collects the appropriate output tax.

HMRC allows overseas sellers making only zero-rated deemed supplies to register or apply for exemption from registration. Import VAT and Customs Duty remain the seller’s responsibility. VAT-registered sellers can reclaim eligible import VAT under the normal recovery rules. Direct sales and sales to VAT-registered business customers can create separate registration obligations, so check the full mix of transactions before applying for exemption.

## What rules apply to VAT rates and Northern Ireland?

Goods sold to UK customers are taxed at standard, reduced, or zero rates depending on product type. Furthermore, Northern Ireland follows European Union VAT rules for physical merchandise under the Windsor Framework. Consignments valued at £135 or less sent from outside the UK and EU to Northern Ireland consumers face border import VAT rather than point-of-sale VAT.

When charging UK VAT, businesses must apply the correct statutory rate based on the products they supply. The standard rate is 20% and covers the majority of consumer merchandise and e-commerce goods. A reduced rate of 5% applies to specific items, such as children's car seats and domestic energy. A zero rate of 0% applies to selected essentials, including books, e-books, basic foodstuffs, and children's clothing.

Sellers trading into Northern Ireland must also account for distinct regional arrangements. Under the Northern Ireland Protocol and the Windsor Framework, Northern Ireland remains aligned with European Union rules for physical goods. This creates differences compared to sales made to customers in England, Scotland, and Wales:

1. **Movements to and from the EU**: Goods transported between Northern Ireland and EU member states are handled under EU intra-Community trading and distance-selling rules, rather than standard UK customs processes.
2. **Low-value imports from outside the UK and EU**: Consignments of goods valued at £135 or less dispatched directly to private consumers in Northern Ireland from outside both the UK and the European Union are subject to import VAT at customs, rather than point-of-sale supply VAT.

Traders operating across both Great Britain and Northern Ireland must configure their storefront checkout systems and logistics processes to account for these regional tax distinctions.

## Preparing your e-commerce operations for UK VAT compliance

To register for UK VAT, businesses submit an application online through HMRC’s VAT Registration Service using a Government Gateway user ID. Overseas applicants can register directly or appoint a UK-based tax representative. HMRC also maintains discretionary powers to request financial securities or require a local representative before granting an overseas registration.

Before taking your first order from a UK buyer, establish which classification applies to your business. If your enterprise is incorporated and managed outside the UK, you trade as a Non-Established Taxable Person with a £0 threshold. Ensure that your storefront accurately identifies customer location, calculates the correct VAT rate at checkout for parcels of £135 or less, and correctly isolates marketplace-facilitated orders from direct sales.
